# Student Interview Script (≈5 minutes)

**Author:** Member 5 (product / validation)
**Purpose:** learn how students *actually* decide where to study, eat, or work
out in gaps between commitments — **before** showing them anything.
**Status:** script is **ready to use**. No interviews have been conducted. Any
results, quotes, or counts are **PENDING** a human teammate running this with
3–5 students. Do not fabricate responses.

## Rules for the interviewer

- Talk to **3–5** BU students, one at a time.
- Ask about **recent, real behavior first**. Do **not** pitch the app until the
  final 60 seconds, and only if time allows.
- **No leading questions.** Never ask "Would you use this amazing app?" or
  "Wouldn't it be great if…". Ask what they did, not what they imagine.
- Keep it anonymous. No names, no BUID, no identifying details recorded.
- Let silences sit; don't fill them with suggestions.
- You may ask "why?" and "can you give a recent example?" as neutral follow-ups.

## Opening (≈30 sec)

> "Thanks for a few minutes. I'm researching how students pick places to study,
> eat, or work out between classes. There are no right answers and this isn't a
> sales pitch — I just want to hear what you actually do. It's anonymous. Okay to
> start?"

## Core questions (≈3.5 min)

1. **Crowding changed a plan (recent & concrete):**
   > "Think about the last time a place being too crowded changed your plans —
   > the gym, a dining hall, a library. What happened?"
   (Follow-up: "What did you do instead?")

2. **How they choose now:**
   > "When you have a gap between classes and want to study, eat, or train, how
   > do you decide where to go right now? Walk me through the last time."

3. **Usable gaps:**
   > "Which gaps in your week are actually usable for something like that —
   > and which are too short to bother?"

4. **Trust:**
   > "If an app told you how busy a place was, what would make you trust that
   > number? What would make you doubt it?"

5. **Pull vs push:**
   > "Would you open a tool like that yourself when you had a gap, or would you
   > only want it to notify you? Why?"

6. **Annoyance:**
   > "What would make suggestions like this annoying enough that you'd stop
   > using it?"

## Optional reveal (≤60 sec, only if time remains)

Show the prototype **without hype**:
> "Here's a rough student project. It shows how busy campus places are on a
> recorded day and suggests quieter times. The campus numbers are **sample
> data**, and one room uses a real historical office dataset. What's your honest
> first reaction — confusing, useful, neither?"

Then stop and record. Do not argue with criticism.

## Close (≈15 sec)

> "That's everything. Thank you — this is really helpful."
